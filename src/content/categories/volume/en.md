## How to use it

1. Type a number into the first field.
2. Pick the units in the lists under the fields — the answer appears in the second field straight away.
3. To convert the other way, press the arrow button between the fields: it swaps the two units.

## How the units relate

| | In metric | In US units |
|---|---|---|
| 1 tablespoon | 14.79 ml | 1/2 fluid ounce |
| 1 fluid ounce | 29.57 ml | 2 tablespoons |
| 1 cup | 236.6 ml | 8 fluid ounces |
| 1 gallon | 3.785 l | 16 cups |
| 1 litre | 1000 ml | 33.8 fluid ounces |
| 1 cubic metre | 1000 l | 264.2 gallons |

## Where each unit turns up

- Millilitres and litres — almost the whole world: drinks, fuel, recipes.
- Cups and tablespoons — American recipes.
- Fluid ounces — drinks, cosmetics and perfume in the US. A can of soda there is 12 ounces, which is 355 ml.
- Gallons — petrol and milk in the US.
- Cubic metres — metered water and gas, concrete, soil.

## Where people slip up

- There is a US gallon and a British one. This is the US gallon, 3.785 litres. The British gallon is 4.546 litres, 20% bigger.
- A cup in a recipe is not any cup off the shelf. A US measuring cup is 236.6 ml, while in Australia and Canada it is metric, 250 ml.
- Tablespoons differ too: 14.8 ml in the US, 20 ml in Australia.
- A fluid ounce is volume, not weight. An ounce of flour and a fluid ounce of water are different things; for weight there is the [weight calculator](/en/tools/weight).

## How the calculator works it out

The calculation runs in your browser: the number is not sent anywhere, and the answer appears at once, with no request to a server. Each unit is first turned into litres, and from litres into the unit you asked for.

A US gallon is defined as 231 cubic inches, which is exactly 3.785411784 litres. The other US units are fractions of it: a cup is 1/16 of a gallon, a fluid ounce 1/128, a tablespoon 1/256. The answer is rounded to six decimal places.
