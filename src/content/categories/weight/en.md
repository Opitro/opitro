## How to use it

1. Type a number into the first field.
2. Pick the units in the lists under the fields — the answer appears in the second field straight away.
3. To convert the other way, press the arrow button between the fields: it swaps the two units.

## How the units relate

| | In metric | In pounds and ounces |
|---|---|---|
| 1 ounce | 28.35 g | 1/16 pound |
| 1 pound | 453.6 g | 16 ounces |
| 1 stone | 6.35 kg | 14 pounds |
| 1 kilogram | 1000 g | 2.205 pounds |
| 1 tonne | 1000 kg | 2204.6 pounds |

## Where each unit turns up

- Kilograms and grams — almost the whole world: food, body weight, luggage.
- Pounds and ounces — the US: body weight, groceries, parcels, dumbbells and plates at the gym.
- Stone — the UK and Ireland, and only for a person's weight.
- Tonnes — freight and machinery.

## Where people slip up

- There is more than one ton. This one is the metric tonne, 1000 kg. The US "short" ton is 907 kg, the British "long" ton is 1016 kg.
- Gold uses a different ounce. Precious metals are weighed in troy ounces of 31.1 g; this is the ordinary ounce, 28.35 g.
- A fluid ounce is volume, not weight. For that there is the [volume calculator](/en/tools/volume).
- Weight in stone is written as two numbers. 11 st 4 lb is 11 × 14 + 4 = 158 pounds, or 71.7 kg.

## How the calculator works it out

The calculation runs in your browser: the number is not sent anywhere, and the answer appears at once, with no request to a server. Each unit is first turned into kilograms, and from kilograms into the unit you asked for.

The factors come from the international agreement of 1959: a pound is exactly 0.45359237 kg, an ounce is 1/16 of a pound, a stone is 14 pounds. The answer is rounded to six decimal places.
