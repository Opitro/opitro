Type a number into the Meters field and the answer appears in the Feet field straight away. Under it there is an "Exact" line with up to six decimal places and a copy icon. The arrow button between the fields opens the reverse conversion, [Feet to Meters](/en/feet-to-m).

You need feet when you know a length in metres but have to give it the way it is done in the US, in aviation or among divers.

## Mountain and flight altitude

- 1000 m — 3281 feet
- 2000 m — 6562 feet
- 3000 m — 9843 feet
- 4808 m, Mont Blanc — 15,774 feet
- 8849 m, Everest — 29,032 feet
- 10,000 m — 32,808 feet

Flight altitude is given in feet all over the world, and flight levels are round numbers in feet: 35,000 feet is 10,668 m.

## Diving depth

Limits for divers are written in both systems and rounded: 18 m is 60 feet, 30 m is 100 feet, 40 m is 130 feet. The exact values are 59.1, 98.4 and 131.2 feet.

## Rooms and plots

Length and width: 3 m is 9.8 feet, 4 m is 13.1 feet, 5 m is 16.4 feet, 6 m is 19.7 feet.

Area is converted with a different number. A square metre is 10.76 square feet, so a 20 m² room is 215 square feet, not 66.

## Height

Height in feet is not written as a decimal. 1.80 m is 5.91 feet, but people say "5 feet 11 inches". There is a ready height table on the [Centimeters to Inches](/en/cm-to-inches) page.

## A quick estimate in your head

Multiply the metres by 3 and add a tenth of the result. 100 m → 300 → plus 30 → 330 feet. The exact value is 328.

## How accurate the answer is

The calculation runs in your browser: the number is not sent anywhere, and the answer appears with no request to a server. Under the international agreement of 1959 a foot is exactly 0.3048 m, so a metre is 3.2808399 feet. The large number in the answer is rounded; the "Exact" line under it shows up to six decimal places.
