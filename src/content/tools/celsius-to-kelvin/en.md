Kelvin is what physics and chemistry problems run on. The gas laws and the formulas of thermodynamics only work with temperature in kelvin: put degrees Celsius in and the answer comes out wrong.

## Why you can't calculate in degrees Celsius

Zero Celsius does not mean "no heat", it is just the freezing point of water. So 40 °C is not twice as hot as 20 °C. In kelvin those are 313.15 and 293.15, a difference of less than 7%. That is exactly how much the pressure of a gas in a sealed cylinder rises when it is heated that far.

## A temperature difference is not converted

A degree Celsius and a kelvin are the same size. If something warms up by 15 °C, it warms up by 15 K. You add 273.15 to a temperature, not to a change in temperature.

## 273 or 273.15

The exact value is 273.15. School problems often use 273 to keep the arithmetic simple. Follow your textbook: the 0.15 usually disappears when the answer is rounded.

## Values that come up in problems

- −273.15 °C — 0 K, absolute zero
- −195.8 °C — 77.35 K, liquid nitrogen boils
- −78.5 °C — 194.65 K, dry ice turns to gas
- 25 °C — 298.15 K, the standard temperature in chemistry tables
- 36.6 °C — 309.75 K
