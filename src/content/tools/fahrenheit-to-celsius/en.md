Type a number into the Fahrenheit field and the answer appears in the Celsius field straight away. The arrow button between the fields opens the reverse conversion, [Celsius to Fahrenheit](/en/celsius-to-fahrenheit): that is the one you need to set a temperature on an American oven or air conditioner.

Fahrenheit turns up in American recipes, in weather forecasts on American sites, on thermometers and in manuals for appliances from the US. The conversions for each case are below.

## The oven, from an American recipe

US recipes give temperatures in 25 °F steps. On an oven marked in Celsius, set:

- 300 °F — 150 °C
- 325 °F — 160 °C
- 350 °F — 180 °C (exactly 177)
- 375 °F — 190 °C
- 400 °F — 200 °C (exactly 204)
- 425 °F — 220 °C
- 450 °F — 230 °C
- 475 °F — 245 °C

## Weather forecast

American forecasts name temperatures by the ten: "in the sixties", "in the nineties". Here is what that means:

- 20–29 °F — −7 to −2 °C, freezing
- 30–39 °F — −1 to 4 °C, around zero
- 40–49 °F — 4 to 9 °C, cold
- 50–59 °F — 10 to 15 °C, cool
- 60–69 °F — 16 to 21 °C, comfortable
- 70–79 °F — 21 to 26 °C, warm
- 80–89 °F — 27 to 32 °C, hot
- 90–99 °F — 32 to 37 °C, very hot

## An American thermometer

In the US a fever starts at 100.4 °F, which is exactly 38 °C. The other marks:

- 97 °F — 36.1 °C
- 98 °F — 36.7 °C
- 99 °F — 37.2 °C
- 100 °F — 37.8 °C
- 101 °F — 38.3 °C
- 102 °F — 38.9 °C
- 103 °F — 39.4 °C
- 104 °F — 40.0 °C

## Fridge and freezer

Two numbers come up in manuals for American appliances and on food packaging. 40 °F is about 4 °C, the upper limit for a fridge. 0 °F is −18 °C, the usual freezer temperature.

## A quick estimate in your head

Subtract 30 and halve it. For 70 °F that gives 20 °C; the exact answer is 21.

Good enough for weather: between 30 and 85 °F you are less than two degrees off. No good for ovens: it turns 350 °F into 160 °C instead of 177.
