Fahrenheit turns up in American recipes, in weather forecasts on American sites, on thermometers and in manuals for appliances from the US. The conversions for each case are below.

If it's the other way round — you need to set a temperature on an American oven or air conditioner — open [Celsius to Fahrenheit](/en/celsius-to-fahrenheit).

## The oven, from an American recipe

US recipes give temperatures in 25 °F steps. On an oven marked in Celsius, set:

- 325 °F — 160 °C
- 350 °F — 180 °C (exactly 177)
- 375 °F — 190 °C
- 400 °F — 200 °C (exactly 204)
- 425 °F — 220 °C
- 450 °F — 230 °C

## Weather forecast

Landmarks that tell you what to wear: 32 °F — zero, water freezes; 50 °F — 10 °C, jacket weather; 70 °F — 21 °C, room temperature; 90 °F — 32 °C, hot; 100 °F — 38 °C, very hot.

## Body temperature

An American thermometer shows tenths, and they shouldn't be rounded:

- 97.9 °F — 36.6 °C
- 98.6 °F — 37.0 °C
- 100.4 °F — 38.0 °C
- 102.2 °F — 39.0 °C
- 104.0 °F — 40.0 °C

## Fridge and freezer

Two numbers come up in manuals for American appliances and on food packaging. 40 °F is about 4 °C, the upper limit for a fridge. 0 °F is −18 °C, the usual freezer temperature.

## A quick estimate in your head

Subtract 30 and halve it. For 70 °F that gives 20 °C; the exact answer is 21.

Good enough for weather: between 30 and 85 °F you are less than two degrees off. No good for ovens: it turns 350 °F into 160 °C instead of 177.
