faq:
  - question: What's the formula for Celsius to Fahrenheit?
    answer: Multiply by 1.8 and add 32 — F = C × 1.8 + 32. For example, 20°C = 68°F.
  - question: What's 180°C (a standard oven temperature) in Fahrenheit?
    answer: 180°C = 356°F (180 × 1.8 + 32) — close to the "350°F" ovens usually get set to for that recipe range.
  - question: Is there a temperature where Celsius and Fahrenheit are the same number?
    answer: Yes, at −40°. That's the one point where −40°C equals −40°F — a neat coincidence of the formula.

Fahrenheit remains the standard scale in the US — weather forecasts, oven temperatures in recipes, thermostat readings. Celsius is used almost everywhere else, including most of Europe.

Handy when reading a US recipe with an oven temperature in Fahrenheit, or trying to figure out how warm it actually is from a US weather forecast.
