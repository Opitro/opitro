Type a number into the Celsius field and the answer appears in the Fahrenheit field straight away. The arrow button between the fields opens the reverse conversion, [Fahrenheit to Celsius](/en/fahrenheit-to-celsius): that is the one you need if the recipe or the forecast is in Fahrenheit.

You need Fahrenheit when you think in Celsius but the thing in front of you doesn't: an oven or air conditioner in an American home, a thermometer bought in the US, a chat about the weather with someone from there. The numbers for each case are below.

## An oven marked in Fahrenheit

American ovens move in 25 °F steps, so you can't set the exact value. Use the nearest mark:

- 160 °C — 325 °F
- 180 °C — 350 °F (exactly 356)
- 190 °C — 375 °F
- 200 °C — 400 °F (exactly 392)
- 220 °C — 425 °F
- 230 °C — 450 °F

## Air conditioner and thermostat

Thermostats don't show fractions, so these are rounded to a whole degree:

- 18 °C — 64 °F
- 20 °C — 68 °F
- 22 °C — 72 °F
- 24 °C — 75 °F
- 26 °C — 79 °F

## Body temperature

Don't round here, the tenths matter:

- 36.6 °C — 97.9 °F
- 37.0 °C — 98.6 °F
- 38.0 °C — 100.4 °F
- 39.0 °C — 102.2 °F
- 40.0 °C — 104.0 °F

## Talking about the weather with an American

You don't need the exact number here, five landmarks are enough:

- 32 °F (0 °C) — water freezes
- 50 °F (10 °C) — cool, jacket weather
- 70 °F (21 °C) — room temperature
- 90 °F (32 °C) — hot
- 100 °F (38 °C) — very hot

## A quick estimate in your head

Double the Celsius figure and add 30. For 20 °C that gives 70 °F; the exact answer is 68.

Good enough for weather: between 0 and 25 °C you are never more than three degrees off. No good for ovens: it turns 180 °C into 390 °F instead of 356.
