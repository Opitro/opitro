Kelvin shows up in answers to problems, in scientific papers and reference tables, and also on light bulb boxes. In the first case you convert to Celsius by subtracting; in the second there is nothing to convert.

For the other direction, see [Celsius to Kelvin](/en/celsius-to-kelvin).

## Kelvin on a light bulb, a monitor and a camera

2700 K, 4000 K, 6500 K are colour temperature: the shade of the light, not heat. The higher the number, the cooler and bluer the light. It is not converted to degrees Celsius.

## Values from physics

- 4.2 K — −268.95 °C, liquid helium boils
- 77 K — −196.15 °C, liquid nitrogen boils
- 300 K — 26.85 °C, "room temperature" in textbook problems

## Very large values

For thousands of kelvin the 273 correction changes almost nothing. The surface of the Sun is about 5800 K, roughly 5500 °C.
