If a physics problem or a space article gives a temperature in Kelvin, this converts it to the Celsius degrees you're used to.

Kelvin shows up a lot in astronomy (star and planet temperatures) and low-temperature physics (superconductors, liquid gases) — it's just more convenient there than dealing with negative Celsius numbers.
