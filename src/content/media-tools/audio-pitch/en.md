faq:
  - question: What's a semitone, roughly?
    answer: It's the smallest step on a piano, one key to the next. Twelve semitones make a full octave. One or two semitones is audible but still sounds natural; a whole octave changes a track beyond recognition.
  - question: Does the tempo or length change?
    answer: No. Only the pitch moves — speed and duration stay exactly the same, which is not what happens if you simply speed playback up or slow it down.
  - question: Why cents as well as semitones?
    answer: A cent is a hundredth of a semitone, so it's a very fine step. It's what you need when a recording sits slightly off-pitch (a guitar that drifted, old tape), when lining up two takes that are a quarter-tone apart, or when retuning a track to A=432 Hz instead of the usual 440. Semitones are far too coarse to catch that.
  - question: How do I find the right key for my voice?
    answer: Start playback, sing along, and nudge the slider a semitone at a time until it sits comfortably. Usually no more than two or three semitones either way is needed.
  - question: Why is there a short pause after moving the slider?
    answer: Changing key while keeping tempo means recalculating the whole recording rather than applying a simple filter, so it takes a couple of seconds. Values you've already heard are remembered, so going back to one is instant.
  - question: What format does it save in?
    answer: WAV, MP3, or OGG, your choice. The download button switches on as soon as you move the slider off zero.

Load a track and move the sliders — semitones for coarse steps, cents for fine tuning — up if it sits too low for you, down if it sits too high. Speed is untouched: the song stays the same length and the same tempo, only the pitch moves.

## Who it's for

- **Fitting a backing track to your voice** — when the original was written for a different range and singing along is a struggle
- **Transposing for an instrument** — shifting a song to a guitar-friendly key without a capo
- **Singing along to someone else's recording** — matching their track to your part
- **Learning a part** — dropping a hard passage lower to work it out, then putting it back

The Original / Result switch under the player compares before and after on the fly, so you can hear whether any artefacts crept in.

This tool only changes musical key. To change how a voice sounds — robot, telephone, radio and other effects — use [voice changer](/en/voice-changer). To change speed without pitch, use [speed](/en/audio-speed). For frequency balance, use the [equalizer](/en/audio-equalizer).
