Enter a number — the written ordinal and the short form appear at once.

## Only the last word changes

**123** becomes “one hundred twenty-third”. The hundred and the twenty stay as they were. The same rule holds in every language on this site, and it is the one most often broken.

## Why 21st but 11th

The teens break the pattern. Eleven, twelve and thirteen all take “th”, even though they end in one, two and three. A rule that looks only at the last digit gets all three wrong.

## The irregular ones are the common ones

Five becomes **fifth**, nine becomes **ninth**, twelve becomes **twelfth**, forty becomes **fortieth**. Adding “th” to everything works for some numbers and fails for exactly the ones people write most.

## No gender here

English ordinals have no gender: the third page and the third floor use the same word. The same page in Russian, Ukrainian or Spanish does have a gender switch, because there the ending changes with the noun.
