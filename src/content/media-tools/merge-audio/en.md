faq:
  - question: How do I change the order of the files?
    answer: With the up and down arrows on the right of each row. Files play in exactly the order they sit in the list, and the number on the left updates straight away. No more removing everything and re-adding it in sequence.
  - question: Can I merge files in different formats or at different quality?
    answer: Yes. Every file is brought to a common sample rate as it's read, and each one's length is preserved exactly. Tested with a 44,100 Hz and a 22,050 Hz file together — three seconds and four seconds came out as three and four.
  - question: What happens at the joins?
    answer: Each seam gets an 80-millisecond crossfade, because a straight butt-join almost always leaves an audible click. That does make the result shorter than the sum of its parts by 0.08 seconds per seam — a 3-second and a 4-second file give you 6.92 seconds, not 7.
  - question: Can I hear the result before downloading it?
    answer: Yes. "Join and listen" assembles the file and draws its waveform, and from there it plays like any other track. Each file in the list can also be played on its own with the green button beside its name, so you can check the order before committing.
  - question: How many files can I join?
    answer: There's no hard limit on the count; the size limit is 1 GB per file. In practice your device's memory is the real ceiling — the joining happens entirely in the browser, so a dozen hour-long recordings may be too much for a phone.
  - question: What format is the result saved in?
    answer: WAV, MP3 or OGG, your choice. WAV is lossless, which is what you want if the file is heading into an editor next. MP3 at 192 kbps is the one for simply sending or listening.
  - question: Do the files get uploaded anywhere?
    answer: No, everything happens right in your browser on your device. Nothing is sent anywhere, and merging still works with no internet connection as long as the page is already open.

Add your files one at a time, put them in order with the arrows, then press "Join and listen" — the finished track's waveform appears and you can play the whole thing. Then pick a format and download.

## What it's good for

- **Assembling a podcast recorded in takes** — intro, conversation and outro as one file
- **Combining voice memos** into a single recording instead of forwarding a dozen
- **Joining audiobook chapters** into one file for the car
- **Reconnecting interview segments** after cutting the unwanted parts out
- **Stitching short recordings** of rehearsals or lessons into one continuous track

Files are joined end to end, not layered over one another: this is sequencing, not mixing several instruments together. If something needs to come out before you join, use [trim audio](/en/trim-audio) or [remove silence](/en/remove-silence). To even out the loudness between parts recorded at different times, try [change volume](/en/audio-volume) or [normalize](/en/normalize-audio).
