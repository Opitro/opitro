faq:
  - question: Will voices go squeaky when I speed things up?
    answer: Not with "Keep the original pitch" ticked, and it's ticked by default. Only the tempo moves; the pitch stays where it was. That's different from simply playing a recording faster, where the voice rises with it.
  - question: What if I actually want the tape-speed effect?
    answer: Untick "Keep the original pitch". Pitch then follows the speed — the classic sound of a cassette wound forward or a record played at the wrong speed.
  - question: How is this different from changing pitch?
    answer: Here the tempo changes, and the length changes with it. On the pitch page it's the opposite — the pitch moves while tempo and length stay put. Different jobs, so different pages.
  - question: How far can I speed up or slow down?
    answer: From 0.25x (four times slower) to 4x (four times faster). In practice speech stays intelligible to around 2x, and music past 1.5x starts to noticeably lose quality.
  - question: Why is there a short pause after changing speed?
    answer: Stretching or compressing a recording without touching its pitch means recalculating the whole file, not just playing it faster. It takes a couple of seconds, and values you've already heard are remembered.
  - question: What format does it save in?
    answer: WAV, MP3, or OGG, your choice. The download button switches on as soon as you change the speed.

Load a file, pick a speed with the slider or a preset button, and listen. Pitch is preserved, so sped-up speech sounds like ordinary speech, just faster — no chipmunk effect.

## Who it's for

- **Getting through a lecture or podcast faster** — 1.25x or 1.5x saves a third of the time and stays perfectly clear
- **Working music out by ear** — slow a tricky run to 0.5x, learn the part, then put it back
- **Transcribing a recording** — 0.75x makes typing along with a dictaphone file far easier
- **Language learning** — slowing a native speaker down until you can hear the individual words
- **Hitting a running time** — fitting a track to the length of a video

"Reset to normal speed" puts everything back to 1x if you overdo it with the slider.

This tool only handles tempo. To move the pitch without touching speed, use [change pitch](/en/audio-pitch). For voice effects, use [voice changer](/en/voice-changer). To cut a recording, use [trim audio](/en/trim-audio).
