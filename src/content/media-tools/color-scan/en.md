Point the camera at an object and you will see its colour in HEX, RGB and HSL. The frame can be frozen and tapped anywhere; a colour can be taken from a photo as well.

## What it is for

You have seen a wall in just the right shade, a cover, a fabric, a sign — and you want that colour on a site or in a layout. Eyes will not repeat it: colour memory is short, and “the same blue” turns out to be a different one an hour later. A camera gives you a number, and a number does not argue.

## How the value is taken

The colour is averaged over a small spot around the point rather than read from one pixel. Every camera has noise, and a single pixel would jump about. The frame stays with you: it is not saved and not sent anywhere.

## What this tool cannot do

It is not a colorimeter. The camera decides white balance on its own, the light in the room adds its own tint, and the same object by a window in the morning and under a lamp in the evening will give different numbers. Good enough for finding a similar colour, not for matching paint exactly.
