A stuck pixel is a subpixel that has stopped switching and frozen in one position. Rapid colour changes force it to switch hundreds of times a minute, and after that shaking it sometimes starts working normally again."

    answer: "Start with noise at 60 Hz: it shakes all three subpixels independently and is the gentlest on the eyes. The strongest shake is black and white, full on and full off, but it is also the harshest to look at. Change the rate too: panels respond differently, and where 60 Hz fails 10 Hz sometimes works."

Choose the patch size, the mode and the time, then press “Start the fix” — the screen turns black and a square of fast flashing colour noise appears in the middle. Drag it so the stuck pixel sits inside, and leave it running. When the time is up the page stops on its own and chimes.

## Stuck pixel or dead pixel

This is half the battle: only one of the two can be fixed.

- **Stuck** — always glows in one colour, red, green or blue. On a black background it is a bright dot. Its transistor is alive but frozen, and it can be shaken loose.
- **Dead** — never lights up. On white it is a black dot, visible on any colour. Flashing is powerless here: there is nothing left to switch.

If you do not know yet which one you have, run the [dead pixel test](/en/dead-pixel-test) first: nine full-screen colours show both the type of defect and exactly where it is.

## How long it takes

Ten minutes is the minimum worth trying. Half an hour succeeds more often, an hour is about the limit for a single session. While it runs the page holds the screen awake, so a phone can simply be left lying next to you.

## What not to do

Do not press the screen with your finger — that advice has ruined more panels than it has saved, and on OLED it is worse still. And do not mistake burn-in for a stuck pixel: if you can make out the ghost of something that sat on screen for a long time, that is burn-in, and flashing will not cure it.
