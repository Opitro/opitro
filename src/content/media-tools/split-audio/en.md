faq:
  - question: Which splitting mode should I use?
    answer: Splitting into equal parts suits you when you want a specific number of files — a concert into four, say. Splitting by length is better when the size of each piece matters, like ten minutes apiece for listening on the move.
  - question: How many parts will I get?
    answer: The line under the settings tells you how many files you'll get and roughly how long each will be. It's worked out from your recording, not guessed.
  - question: How are the files inside the archive named?
    answer: After your original file with a number appended, zero-padded — so players and file managers sort them properly rather than as 1, 10, 2.
  - question: Why an archive instead of separate downloads?
    answer: Browsers won't hand over a dozen files from one press without asking you to confirm each. An archive is the only way to deliver them all at once.
  - question: Will the parts join back up without gaps?
    answer: Yes — the parts run strictly back to back, with no sample lost or repeated. Join them again with [merge audio](/en/merge-audio) and you get the original recording.
  - question: Can it split at pauses instead of by time?
    answer: Not yet — splitting is by time. If your goal is removing pauses rather than cutting at them, see [remove silence](/en/remove-silence).
  - question: What format are the parts saved in?
    answer: MP3 or WAV, your choice. MP3 is written at 192 kbps, which is plenty for speech and music alike and makes for a much lighter archive.

Load a file, pick how to split it and enter a number. The line under the settings shows how many parts you'll get and how long they'll be. Then choose a format and download the archive.

## What it's good for

- **A long lecture or audiobook** — split into chapters by time
- **A concert or rehearsal recording** — broken out into separate pieces
- **A file that's too big to send** — mail and messengers often cap at 20–25 MB
- **Transcription** — many services accept only a limited length
- **Uploading in pieces** where there's a duration limit

The reverse job is [merge audio](/en/merge-audio). If you want one specific section rather than cutting the whole thing up, that's [trim audio](/en/trim-audio).
