Press play and drag the slider — the tone follows without clicks. If you need an exact frequency, type it into the big number or nudge it a hertz at a time. Waveform, volume and channel all switch while the sound is running, so you can compare by ear rather than from memory.

## What is here

- **Sine, square, sawtooth, triangle** — switched on the fly.
- **A logarithmic slider**: equal movements give equal steps to the ear, so hitting 50 Hz is as easy as hitting 5000.
- **Note and cents** under the number, for tuning an instrument.
- **Channels separately** — to find the silent earpiece.
- **An oscilloscope** — the real wave from the output, not a picture.

## The presets and what they are for

**440 Hz** is concert A, the reference every instrument is tuned from. **1000 Hz** is the standard reference tone for almost every audio measurement. **50 Hz** is mains hum: if you hear something like it from your speakers on their own, look at the earthing or the cable. **4000 Hz** is where the ear is most sensitive. **15,000 Hz** is roughly the upper limit of adult hearing.

## Careful with the volume

Start quiet. Square and sawtooth sound noticeably louder than a sine at the same setting because of their overtones. In headphones, high frequencies at full volume damage hearing for real rather than in theory: it may not even feel unpleasant while the damage is being done.
