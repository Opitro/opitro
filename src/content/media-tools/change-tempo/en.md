  - question: How accurate is the tempo detection?
    answer: On music with a steady rhythmic base, usually accurate. It looks for repeating peaks in loudness, so it rarely misses on dance and pop, while a live recording with no firm beat, speech, or classical may give nothing at all or land on half or double the value. You can always correct the number by hand.
  - question: Why did it show double or half the tempo?
    answer: That's the usual ambiguity — 70 and 140 beats per minute describe the same rhythm, just counted differently. If the number looks odd, halve it or double it.
  - question: Does the key change?
    answer: No. It uses the same stretching algorithm as the speed page — the tempo moves and the pitch stays. This is not the same as playing the track faster.
  - question: How is this different from changing the speed?
    answer: Same job, different unit. The speed page takes a percentage; this one takes beats per minute. If you're matching two tracks or lining music up to a metronome, BPM is the easier way to think.
  - question: How far can I push it?
    answer: A 10–15% change passes unnoticed. Beyond that you start hearing artefacts on the attacks — that's true of any pitch-preserving stretch. Listen to the result before downloading.
  - question: Why is the download button greyed out?
    answer: While the current and target tempos match there's nothing to change. A difference of even one beat turns it on.

Load a track, press "Detect the tempo" and type in the tempo you want. The line under the fields shows how much the speed changes and what the new length will be. Listen, then download.

## What it's good for

- **Matching two tracks** to a common tempo for a mix
- **Slowing a difficult passage** to learn it, without dropping the key
- **Fitting music to a video** of a set length
- **Practising to a metronome** — pulling a recording to a round tempo
- **Checking the BPM** of a track you bought or downloaded

This tool only deals with tempo. To move the pitch, use [change pitch](/en/audio-pitch). To set speed as a percentage, use [change speed](/en/audio-speed).
