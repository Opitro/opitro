faq:
  - question: Which video formats work?
    answer: MP4, MOV, WebM, MKV, AVI and other common containers. The video itself isn't re-encoded — the audio track is simply lifted out — so this is quicker than converting video.
  - question: Can I hear the audio before saving it?
    answer: Yes. Once the file loads a waveform is drawn, and the green button plays the track — so you can tell at a glance whether there's any sound in there and whether it's the right clip.
  - question: Which format should I pick?
    answer: MP3 is universal and opens anywhere. M4A gives the same quality in a smaller file and suits Apple devices. WAV is uncompressed, for when the file is heading into an editor. MP3 and M4A also let you set a bitrate.
  - question: What does the loudness checkbox do?
    answer: It brings the recording to −16 LUFS, the broadcast level podcasts and streaming platforms target. Useful when the audio in a video was recorded too quietly or jumps in level between scenes.
  - question: Does extracting hurt the quality?
    answer: The audio in a video is already compressed, and saving to MP3 or M4A compresses it a second time — a small loss, but a real one. WAV adds no further lossy step.
  - question: Does anything happen to my video?
    answer: No. The file on your device is untouched — a copy of its audio is read out, and all of it happens in your browser with nothing uploaded.
  - question: What's the largest video I can load?
    answer: 1 GB. Files under 150 MB process quickly; beyond that it depends on your device's memory, and a heavy video may be too much for a phone.

Load a video — a waveform and a play button appear so you can check the track. Pick a format and press Extract the audio.

## What it's good for

- **Pulling a song** out of a music video
- **A podcast published only as video** — listen in a player without the picture
- **A voice note filmed as video** that needs to go out as audio
- **Lecture or webinar audio** for listening on the move
- **Material for an edit** — take the track as WAV and work on it separately

From there you can do anything with the sound: [remove noise](/en/denoise-audio), [trim the part you need](/en/trim-audio), [make a ringtone](/en/ringtone), or [compress the file](/en/compress-audio).
