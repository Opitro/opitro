You can remove water from the speaker of any phone — an iPhone, a Samsung, a Xiaomi or anything else — and from earbuds and speakers as well: the sound comes out of the very place it has to clear. Take the case off, turn the volume all the way up, point the speaker downwards and press the big button. Fifteen seconds is enough for a first pass; if the sound is still crackly, shake the phone, blot it with a tissue and run it again.

## How it works

Sound in a speaker is made by the cone moving. The lower the frequency and the louder the sound, the further it travels — down low it moves a fraction of a millimetre, and that is your pump. A droplet is held in the grille by surface tension, and a jolt of air breaks it loose.

Hence:

- **for water** — short pulses at 150–250 Hz rather than a steady drone: a droplet sits a steady tone out;
- **for dust** — a slow sweep upwards: particles of different sizes come loose at different frequencies.

## What not to do

- **Do not charge a wet phone.** Current in a flooded port kills more phones than the water itself. Modern models block charging on their own — that protection is not there to be bypassed.
- **Do not use a hairdryer or a radiator.** Hot air drives moisture deeper under the boards and softens the adhesive that seals the phone.
- **Do not put it in rice.** It pulls no water out of a sealed body, and it does leave starchy dust in the ports.

## Honestly about what this does

Sound clears water out of the speaker grille, and that is all. It does not dry out what got inside the body and it does not repair a flooded board. If the phone went for a full swim, this page will clear the crackle but will not replace proper drying and a repair shop. Crackle after water can linger for a few hours while the last of it evaporates; if the sound has not come back within a day, water is no longer the problem.
