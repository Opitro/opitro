faq:
  - question: How is this different from merging?
    answer: Merging puts files one after another — first one, then the next. A mixer lays them over each other so they play at the same time. Voice-over with music needs the mixer.
  - question: Which file is the main one?
    answer: The first in the list — it starts at 100%. But there's no rigid split into main track and bed — every file has its own level slider right in its row, so any of them can be pushed above or below the others. Reorder with the arrows, same as in merge.
  - question: What level should I use?
    answer: The voice stays at 100% and the other files start at 35%, which is usually a good starting point for music under speech. If the voice gets lost, come down to 20–25. Listen at the volume your audience will use — music always seems less present when you're monitoring quietly than it really is.
  - question: How long is the result?
    answer: As long as the longest track. Short music under a long voice track will end early and leave silence after it — if you need it throughout, [loop it](/en/loop-audio) first.
  - question: Can summing the tracks overload?
    answer: Adding signals together can exceed full scale, which would distort. We check for that, and if the sum goes over the ceiling the whole mix is scaled down proportionally — you get slightly quieter rather than crunchy.
  - question: Can the music duck automatically under the voice?
    answer: No, there's no automatic ducking here — a file's level is constant for its whole length. That's noticeably simpler than what editing software does, but it covers most jobs.

Add your files one at a time. Each gets its own level slider right in its row: leave the voice as it is, bring the music down, push a third track up. Press "Mix and listen" and judge the result before downloading.

## What it's good for

- **Voice-over with background music** for a video
- **A podcast with a music bed** under the intro
- **Meditation or an audio guide** — voice over a calm background
- **A demo** — vocals over a backing track
- **An audio greeting** — a message over music
