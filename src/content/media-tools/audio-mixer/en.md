faq:
  - question: How is this different from merging?
    answer: Merging puts files one after another — first one, then the next. A mixer lays them over each other so they play at the same time. Voice-over with music needs the mixer.
  - question: Which file is the main one?
    answer: The first in the list. It plays at full level and everything after it goes underneath at whatever level you set. Reorder with the arrows, same as in merge.
  - question: What level should the bed be?
    answer: The default is 35%, which is usually a good starting point for music under speech. If the voice gets lost, come down to 20–25. Listen at the volume your audience will use — music always seems less present when you're monitoring quietly than it really is.
  - question: How long is the result?
    answer: As long as the longest track. Short music under a long voice track will end early and leave silence after it — if you need it throughout, [loop it](/en/loop-audio) first.
  - question: Can summing the tracks overload?
    answer: Adding signals together can exceed full scale, which would distort. We check for that, and if the sum goes over the ceiling the whole mix is scaled down proportionally — you get slightly quieter rather than crunchy.
  - question: Can the music duck automatically under the voice?
    answer: No, there's no automatic ducking here — the bed level is constant throughout. That's noticeably simpler than what editing software does, but it covers most jobs.

Add your files one at a time — the first becomes the main track and the rest go underneath it. Set the bed level, press "Mix and listen", and judge the result before downloading.

What it's good for:

- **Voice-over with background music** for a video
- **A podcast with a music bed** under the intro
- **Meditation or an audio guide** — voice over a calm background
- **A demo** — vocals over a backing track
- **An audio greeting** — a message over music

Nearby: [merge audio](/en/merge-audio) if the files should follow each other, [loop](/en/loop-audio) to stretch short music, and [even out the levels](/en/dynamic-compressor) on the voice before mixing.
