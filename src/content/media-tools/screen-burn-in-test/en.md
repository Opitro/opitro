  - question: What is the difference between burn-in and image retention?
    answer: "Image retention is temporary: a faint trace of what was on screen a moment ago. It fades by itself in minutes or hours, or in a single session with the evening-out mode, and it happens even on ordinary LCDs. Burn-in is permanent: the emitters under a navigation bar or a channel logo have worked far more than their neighbours and now shine dimmer. That is wear, and it cannot be undone. The test is the same — the difference is whether the mark goes away."
  - question: How do I check a second-hand phone before buying?
    answer: "Open this page on the phone itself, turn brightness to maximum and switch auto-brightness off. Step through the fills from dark grey to light grey and white, looking straight on. Watch the places where something always sits: the navigation bar at the bottom, the status bar at the top, the camera cutout. If a faint ghost of icons or a bar shows through, the screen is worn and there is room to haggle."
  - question: Why check on grey rather than black?
    answer: "On black an OLED simply switches pixels off, so no difference in wear shows at all. Burn-in appears as uneven brightness between neighbouring areas, and you can only see that where the pixels light up evenly and not too brightly. That is why the grey ladder comes first, with white and pure colours after it: those show which of the three emitters has aged most."
  - question: Does evening out really repair the screen?
    answer: "No, and that should be said plainly. It clears image retention completely — that trace would have faded anyway, just slower. Real burn-in cannot be fixed: an emitter does not recover. The bands only age the neighbouring pixels a little closer to the worn ones, which softens the edge of the patch. The screen wears further while it runs, so leaving it going for days is both pointless and harmful."
  - question: How long should evening out run?
    answer: "Ten minutes is enough for fresh image retention. If the mark has been there a while, an hour or two makes sense. Longer is not worth it: if nothing has changed in two hours it is burn-in, and time will not beat that. The screen will not sleep during the session — the page keeps it awake."
  - question: Can an LCD monitor burn in?
    answer: "Not in the real sense: LCD pixels do not emit light themselves, a shared backlight does. It can get image retention — a long still picture makes the crystals stick — but the trace fades by itself within hours, faster with the bands. Uneven brightness at the edges is backlight bleed, which has nothing to do with burn-in."
  - question: How do I keep a screen from burning in?
    answer: "Turn on auto-hiding for the navigation bar and use a dark theme, set the screen to sleep after a minute or two, and keep brightness moderate. What wears a screen is bright, motionless elements: the white background of a messenger, a channel logo, a game interface. TVs have built-in pixel shifting — better left switched on."

The check runs on flat fills: press a colour and the screen fills with it edge to edge; pressing again moves to the next. Start with the greys — they show burn-in best. If a mark turns up, switch to the “Even out” tab: slow bands of every colour take over the screen, and each pixel takes its turn under each of them.

## What you will see

- **A faint ghost of icons, a navigation bar or a logo** — burn-in or image retention; the only difference is whether the mark fades
- **A yellowish patch** — quite normal: the blue emitter ages faster than red and green, so worn areas drift towards yellow
- **Uneven brightness at the edges** — backlight bleed on an LCD, nothing to do with burn-in
- **A single dot** — not burn-in but a dead or stuck pixel; those have [their own test](/en/dead-pixel-test) and [their own fix](/en/stuck-pixel-fixer)

## Look at it properly

Turn brightness all the way up and switch auto-brightness off: a faint mark is invisible on a dim screen. Look straight on — at an angle the panel shifts colour by itself. And wipe the screen: finger smears on a grey fill look more convincing than any burn-in.

## Honestly about evening out

It clears image retention and does not repair burn-in. An emitter that has worked twice as long as its neighbours does not come back — the bands merely pull the surrounding pixels towards its state, which blurs the edge of the patch. If nothing has changed in an hour or two, there is no point continuing: you are only wearing the screen down further.
