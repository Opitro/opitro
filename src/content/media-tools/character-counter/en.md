Paste or type your text — the numbers count themselves on every keystroke. Nothing to press, and the text goes nowhere.

## What is counted

- **Characters with spaces** — the headline number, the one usually asked for
- **Characters without spaces** — the same thing, letters and marks only
- **Words** — chunks between spaces; a hyphen does not split a word
- **Sentences** — by full stops, question and exclamation marks
- **Lines and paragraphs** — a paragraph is a chunk separated by an empty line

Next to that you get the reading time, aloud and silently, and whether the text fits the familiar limits: a post on X, one SMS, a search title and description, a Telegram post and an Instagram caption.

## What it is usually for

Character counts come up where the volume is paid for or capped: a copywriter is asked for “two thousand characters with spaces”, a student for an essay length, a marketplace seller for a product description, a social media manager for a post that will not be cut off.

**SMS** is a story of its own. A message holds 160 characters in Latin script but only 70 in Cyrillic, because the encoding is different. The counter works this out from your own text, so the number is always honest.

## Worth knowing

On a long text the gap between “with spaces” and “without” is about a sixth — on ten thousand characters that is fifteen hundred. If money depends on the number, it is worth asking which one is meant.
