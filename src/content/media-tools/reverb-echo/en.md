  - question: What's the difference between echo and reverb?
    answer: Echo is distinct repeats you could count. Reverb is thousands of tiny reflections blurred into a single tail — the sound of the room itself. The first two styles here are echo; the other two are genuine convolution reverb rather than a repeating delay.
  - question: Which style should I pick?
    answer: Echo gives a clear repeat 0.28 seconds later, audible as a separate voice. Slapback is a very short repeat at 0.09 seconds — the rockabilly and old-studio effect, adding thickness without smearing speech. Room is a small space with a tail of about a second. Hall is a large space with a tail approaching three seconds.
  - question: Can this remove echo from a recording?
    answer: No, and it never could — the effect is added, not taken away. Removing room echo that's already recorded is extremely hard even in professional software, and we're not going to pretend otherwise.
  - question: Will speech stay intelligible?
    answer: With slapback and room, yes. Hall smears the ends of words, so it suits vocals, instruments and sound design far better than a podcast.
  - question: Does the file get longer?
    answer: No, the duration stays the same. That means the very tail of the effect is cut off with the end of the file — if the tail matters, add a little silence at the end beforehand.

## What it's good for

- **Vocals that sound dry** — room adds the naturalness of not having sung in a closet
- **Sound design** — hall makes a voice cavernous and distant
- **A retro sound** — slapback is the signature trick of fifties records
- **A rhythmic effect** — echo with an audible repeat works as part of the arrangement

Straight about the method: the reverb here is built from a synthesised room response — decaying noise, the standard approach when there's no recording of a real hall to work from. It sounds like a genuine space, but it isn't a fingerprint of one particular room.
