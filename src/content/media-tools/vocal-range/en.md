faq:
  - question: How should I sing for an accurate reading?
    answer: A slow scale from your lowest comfortable note up to your highest, holding each note for a second or so. Don't strain into a shout at the top or a rasp at the bottom — notes you can't hold steadily aren't really part of your working range anyway.
  - question: Why are the very extremes discarded?
    answer: On purpose. One cracked note or a click from the microphone would otherwise decide the whole result. The tool takes its bounds slightly inside the extremes, so it reports the range you actually hold.
  - question: Can I record right here?
    answer: There's no record button on this page, but the [voice recorder](/en/dictaphone) is next door — record there, download, and load it here. Either way it all happens on your own device.
  - question: Will a recording with music behind it work?
    answer: No. One pitch is detected at a time, and accompaniment throws it off. You need a clean voice with no music. If all you have is a recording with music, re-recording is simpler than trying to separate it.
  - question: What do semitones and octaves mean?
    answer: A semitone is the smallest step between neighbouring notes, and there are twelve to an octave. Two octaves — 24 semitones — is a good amateur range; trained singers often reach three or more.
  - question: Does this tell me my voice type — tenor, soprano?
    answer: Not on its own. Voice type depends on timbre and on where your voice sits comfortably, not just on how far it reaches. Range is one part of the picture, though a substantial one.

Load a recording of singing and the tool works through it note by note, reporting the lowest and highest you sing and how many semitones and octaves that covers.

## What it's good for

- **Picking a key** that suits your voice rather than fighting someone else's
- **Tracking progress** — measure once a month and watch the edges move
- **Choosing repertoire** that sits in the comfortable part of your range
- **Working out how far to transpose** a backing track
