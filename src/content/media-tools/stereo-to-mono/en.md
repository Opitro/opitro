faq:
  - question: What's the difference between the three modes?
    answer: Mixing both channels adds left and right together and halves the result, so nothing is lost. Left-only and right-only take one channel and discard the other. Those two matter when the channels hold different things — an interview with the host on one mic and the guest on the other, for instance.
  - question: What happens to sounds panned to one side?
    answer: When mixing, anything that sat left or right ends up centred. For speech and single-mic recordings you'll barely hear a difference; music will lose its sense of width.
  - question: Can the audio get quieter after folding down?
    answer: It can, and that's expected. If the same sound was recorded out of phase between the channels, adding them cancels part of it — rare, but it's exactly how a phase problem in the original recording shows itself. Use the play button to check the result before downloading.
  - question: Does the file get smaller?
    answer: In WAV, exactly half — each channel takes its own space there. In MP3 the saving is smaller, usually a quarter to a third, because the codec already economises on similar channels.
  - question: Why use mono at all?
    answer: Phone and voice systems, single-mic podcasts, audio for smart speakers, and anything else where there's no stereo effect to hear but you're paying for it in file size.
  - question: What if the file is already mono?
    answer: The tool says so, right under the setting. Converting it again achieves nothing — you'd get the same audio back.

Load a file, pick a mode and press play — you'll hear the folded-down version. Then pick a format and download.

## What it's good for

- **A podcast recorded on one mic** — the stereo track is just taking up space
- **Interviews with split channels** — keep only the speaker you need
- **Voice prompts and IVR** for phone systems that expect mono
- **Cutting file size** when there's no stereo effect in the recording anyway
