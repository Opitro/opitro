faq:
  - question: Why add silence at all?
    answer: Most often so a recording doesn't begin on the very first syllable. Half a second at the front gives a player time to get going and the listener time to be ready. At the end, a pause stops a reverb tail or a final word being clipped as the next file starts.
  - question: How much can I add?
    answer: Up to 60 seconds on each side. The resulting file length appears under the fields as you type.
  - question: Is it true silence or very quiet noise?
    answer: True silence, actual zeros. The original audio isn't touched at all — it's simply shifted along by however many seconds you asked for.
  - question: Does the quality suffer?
    answer: The insertion itself costs nothing. Any loss comes from encoding the result, so take the WAV if the file is heading into an editor.
  - question: What if I need to remove pauses instead?
    answer: That's [remove silence](/en/remove-silence) — it cuts the pauses automatically and tells you how much came out.
  - question: Why is the download button greyed out?
    answer: While both fields sit at zero there's nothing to add and the output would be a copy of the input. Type some seconds and the button turns on.

Load a file, say how much silence goes at the start and the end, listen, and download. The resulting length is shown under the fields.

## What it's good for

- **A ringtone or notification** that cuts off too abruptly
- **A track for a video edit** that needs shifting in time
- **Audiobooks and podcasts** — even gaps between chapters
- **Prep for effects** — [echo and reverb](/en/reverb-echo) lose their tail at the end of a file, and some spare silence solves that
- **A run-up before the start** of a voice recording
