Drop in a photo and get the text: the reading happens right in the browser, on your device. Every line found is outlined on the picture itself, so you can see at a glance what was read and what was missed.

## What you can take away

The text copies with one button or saves as a .txt file. Separately there is a PDF with a text layer: the photo looks as usual, but the text inside can be searched and selected — handy for contracts and scans you will later need to find by words.

## When it reads badly

The reader deals with printed letters, not handwriting. A blurred frame, strongly slanted lines, shadows across the page and small type on a busy background all get in its way. Three things that nearly always help: get closer, turn the photo with the button if the page lay sideways, and box off just the part you need so the engine is not distracted by the rest.

## Why the photo goes nowhere

The reading program comes into your browser and runs on your device. The page has no server of its own, so there is nowhere to send the photo — and that matters most where people bring other people's letters, passports and contracts.
