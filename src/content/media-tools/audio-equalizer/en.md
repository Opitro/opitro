Load a song, start playback, and move the sliders — the sound changes as you go, with nothing to press in between. You hear the difference immediately rather than after processing, so you can just tune it by ear until it sounds right.

What you can do:

- **Add bass** — raise the 31-125 Hz bands to make a track fuller and punchier on headphones or speakers
- **Cut boominess** — pull down 250-500 Hz when a recording sounds muddy, like it was made in a barrel
- **Bring a voice forward** — raise 1-2 kHz so words come through clearly in a song or interview
- **Brighten a dull track** — add air and sparkle at 8-16 kHz
- **Tame harshness** — pull down 2-4 kHz when the high end feels sharp and tiring to listen to

Each of the ten bands covers its own slice of the spectrum, adjustable within 12 dB either way in 0.5 dB steps, and every slider is labelled with what it actually affects — no need to know your way around hertz. "Reset equalizer" puts everything back to zero if you overdo it.

Don't feel like adjusting by hand? Take a preset: Bass Boost, Vocal, Rock, Classical, Movie, Radio. Tap one, all sliders jump into position, then fine-tune from there however you like.

This tool does frequency balance and nothing else. To remove noise, even out loudness, or clean a recording up overall, use [Improve Sound](/en/audio-enhancer). To change format, use the [audio converter](/en/audio-converter). Volume, speed, and pitch each have their own tool in the audio section.
