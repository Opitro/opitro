## Three forms, not six cases

English numerals have no cases: nothing changes with the role of the number in a sentence. What changes is the form.

- **Cardinal** — for counting: five hundred forty-two
- **Ordinal** — for order: five hundred forty-second
- **Formal, on a cheque** — five hundred and forty-two

## Only the last word becomes ordinal

“Five hundred forty-second”, never “fifth hundredth forty-second”. And most of those words are irregular: five becomes fifth, twelve becomes twelfth, forty becomes fortieth. Adding “th” to everything gets several of them wrong.

## The hyphen rule

Hyphens go between tens and units only: forty-two, ninety-nine. Hundreds are not hyphenated to the rest — “five hundred forty-two”, not “five-hundred-forty-two”. Hyphenating the whole number is common and incorrect.

## Why no case table here

Cases belong to Russian, Ukrainian, German, Latin. English has none for numerals, and inventing a table would help nobody. The same page in Russian or Ukrainian does show one.
