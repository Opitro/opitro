faq:
  - question: How is this different from normalizing?
    answer: Normalizing multiplies the whole recording by one number — quiet and loud keep the same relationship, everything just gets louder. A compressor is selective, holding down only the loud moments so the whole thing can come up afterwards. This is what evens out the swings; normalizing doesn't.
  - question: How is it different from changing the volume?
    answer: There you move the level of the entire file by hand and it's easy to overshoot into distortion. Here the loud peaks are held down automatically, so the recording gets steadier and denser rather than simply louder.
  - question: Which mode should I pick?
    answer: Voice is for one person talking, which is most recordings. Podcast is gentler, so breaths and pauses don't push forward. Music is the most delicate and leaves the dynamics largely intact. Heavy is for the extreme case where someone alternates between whispering and shouting.
  - question: Will it sound unnatural?
    answer: On Heavy it certainly can. Compression always costs some life, and the harder it works the more obvious that is. Start on Voice, compare against the original with the play button, and only move up if you can hear that it isn't enough.
  - question: Does it remove background noise?
    answer: No — the opposite. Quiet material comes up along with the wanted signal, so noise becomes more audible. If there's a steady background, run [noise reduction](/en/denoise-audio) first and compress afterwards.
  - question: What should I do with the recording afterwards?
    answer: Usually [normalize](/en/normalize-audio) it — the compressor evens out the swings and normalizing lifts the result to the ceiling without overloading. Together they give you audio that's both steady and loud.

Load a recording, pick a mode and press play — what you hear is already processed. Compare it against the original, then download.

What it's good for:

- **Interviews where one person is loud and the other is quiet**
- **Lecture recordings** where the speaker keeps moving towards and away from the mic
- **Voice-over for video** that you keep having to adjust through the edit
- **Voice messages** where half the words get lost
- **Prepping for publication** — platforms prefer audio that holds a steady level

Order matters. [Remove noise](/en/denoise-audio) first, even out the levels here, then [normalize](/en/normalize-audio). Do it the other way round and the compressor lifts the noise along with the voice.
